# Receipts and tax time

FlyBudget keeps your receipts with the transactions they prove, and lets you mark transactions as
**tax deductions** for your Australian tax return. At tax time, **Tax time** lists the financial
year's deductions by return label, with their receipts, ready to download for you or your tax agent.

This is record keeping, not tax advice. Whether something is deductible is up to you and your tax
agent.

## Receipts

Receipts work for any purchase, not just tax. **Transactions → Receipts** lists every receipt,
matched or not. Upload photos or PDFs (several at once), drop or paste them, or use **Scan
receipt**, which opens the camera on a phone. Photos are shrunk to a sensible size before they are
saved.

Open a receipt to type what it says: merchant, date, amount, and optionally subtotal, tax, tip and
fees, and its **line items**, each with a category. Then it is **matched** to its transaction:

- **Automatically**, to money out of exactly its amount: from the same merchant up to 2 days before
  or 7 days after the receipt's date (card purchases often post late), or any merchant within a
  day. When two transactions could fit, FlyBudget doesn't guess: you choose. A receipt that arrives
  before its transaction is matched when the transaction arrives from your bank.
- **Find match:** search your transactions by payee or notes, and match it yourself.
- **Add transaction:** for cash, or an account without bank sync, make the transaction from the
  receipt. Don't do this if your bank will send it, or you'll have it twice.

A matched transaction keeps its amount. It is tagged **Receipt**, gets the receipt's notes and
items in its notes, and, with **autosplit**, is split by its items' categories when it has no category yet: tax in proportion to each category's items, tip and fees shared evenly per item. A transaction that already has a category or a tax claim is not split by itself: open the receipt and choose **Split by items** (a claimed one can't be split, or its claim would be lost). A receipt with one category sets an
uncategorised transaction's category, but never changes one you chose. Turn each of these on or off
in the Receipts tab's **settings**. **Unmatch** puts the receipt back; deleting a receipt never
deletes its transaction.

### Reading receipts automatically

FlyBudget can fill in a photo's details for you with a vision model running on your own
[Ollama](https://ollama.com) server, such as the Ollama app on Home Assistant. Receipts never leave
your network.

1. In Ollama, add a model that can read images. On a small server, `qwen3-vl:4b-instruct` is a
   good start. It needs about 4 to 5 GB of memory while it reads, and frees it a few minutes later.
   Avoid "thinking" variants, which are much slower on a CPU.
2. In the Receipts tab's **settings**, enter the Ollama address (on Home Assistant, the address on
   its internal network from the Ollama app's documentation) and choose **Check**.
3. Choose the model, then **Try a sample receipt** to see how well and how fast it reads a
   made-up receipt. Nothing in your budget changes.
4. Tick **Read new photos when they are added**.

New photos are then read in the background, one at a time. On a mini PC without a graphics card
this takes a minute or more each. Only empty fields are filled, and line items only when there are
none, so anything you typed is kept. Then the receipt is matched as usual. Open it to check what
was read. **Read again** asks once more. PDFs aren't read.

What was read is checked before it's kept. If the items don't add up to the total, they aren't
saved, so a matched payment isn't split by wrong amounts; the receipt says so and you can add
them by hand or **Read again**. A card terminal (EFTPOS, Tyro, Square) is never taken for the
shop, and tax or fees above the total are left out.

If a model keeps getting receipts wrong:

- Photograph the receipt flat, in good light, filling the frame, with no other paper in view.
  Long receipts read better in two photos than one tiny one.
- Use **Try a sample receipt** to compare models. A larger model, such as `qwen2.5vl:7b`, reads
  more accurately but takes about twice as long and needs about 6 to 8 GB of memory.
- Typing the total and date yourself is enough for matching; reading then only fills the rest.

You can also add a receipt straight from a transaction's details. A paperclip in the register shows
which transactions have one. **Tax time → Receipt inbox** shows the unmatched ones too.

Receipts are stored in your budget's database. Backups made from **Settings** carry each receipt's
details but not the file itself, so they stay small; restoring on the same computer keeps every
file. To keep the files elsewhere, download a year's **CSV and receipts (ZIP)**. A full copy of
the database (the Home Assistant backup, for example) includes them.

## Tax deductions

In a transaction's details, tick **Claim as a tax deduction**, then choose its label and how much
of it was for work:

| Label | Covers                                                      |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| D1    | Work-related car expenses                                   |
| D2    | Work-related travel expenses                                |
| D3    | Work-related clothing, laundry and dry-cleaning             |
| D4    | Work-related self-education expenses                        |
| D5    | Other work-related expenses (tools, phone, home office)     |
| D6    | Low-value pool deduction                                    |
| D7    | Interest deductions                                         |
| D8    | Dividend deductions                                         |
| D9    | Gifts or donations                                          |
| D10   | Cost of managing tax affairs                                |
| D15   | Other deductions (income protection insurance, for example) |

A phone used 60% for work is claimed at 60%. A split transaction is claimed part by part, so the
work half of a shop can be claimed on its own.

**Rules can claim for you:** add the action **Claim as tax deduction** to a rule (for example, payee
contains "Officeworks") and matching payments are claimed as they import. A rule never replaces a
claim you made yourself.

## Tax time

**Tax time** shows one financial year (1 July to 30 June) at a time: the total claimed, the total
by label, and every claim with its cost, work-use share and receipts. It points out what a tax
agent would ask about:

- **No receipt:** once your work-related expenses pass $300 for the year, the ATO expects written
  evidence for each of them.
- **Over $300:** an item costing more than $300 is usually claimed as decline in value over several
  years rather than all at once.

**Deductions (CSV)** downloads the list; **CSV and receipts (ZIP)** adds every receipt file, named
by date, label and payee. Keep receipts for five years from when you lodge your return.
