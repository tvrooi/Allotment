# Receipts and tax time

FlyBudget keeps your receipts with the transactions they prove, and lets you mark transactions as
**tax deductions** for your Australian tax return. At tax time, **Tax time** lists the financial
year's deductions by return label, with their receipts, ready to download for you or your tax agent.

This is record keeping, not tax advice. Whether something is deductible is up to you and your tax
agent.

## Receipts

Open a transaction and use **Receipts** to add a photo or PDF: choose a file (on a phone this offers
the camera), drop one on the box, or paste a screenshot. Photos are shrunk to a sensible size before
they are saved. A paperclip in the register shows which transactions have one.

Got the receipt before the bank transaction? Add it to **Tax time → Receipt inbox**. Type its date
and total, and the inbox suggests the transaction it belongs to (an exact total first). Choose
**Attach** when it arrives. Deleting a transaction moves its receipts back to the inbox.

Receipts are stored in your budget's database. Backups made from **Settings** carry each receipt's
details but not the file itself, so they stay small; restoring on the same computer keeps every
file. To keep the files elsewhere, download a year's **CSV and receipts (ZIP)**. A full copy of
the database (the Home Assistant backup, for example) includes them.

## Tax deductions

In a transaction's details, tick **Claim as a tax deduction**, then choose its label and how much
of it was for work:

| Label | Covers                                                      |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| D1    | Work-related car expenses                                   |
| D2    | Work-related travel expenses                                |
| D3    | Work-related clothing, laundry and dry-cleaning             |
| D4    | Work-related self-education expenses                        |
| D5    | Other work-related expenses (tools, phone, home office)     |
| D6    | Low-value pool deduction                                    |
| D7    | Interest deductions                                         |
| D8    | Dividend deductions                                         |
| D9    | Gifts or donations                                          |
| D10   | Cost of managing tax affairs                                |
| D15   | Other deductions (income protection insurance, for example) |

A phone used 60% for work is claimed at 60%. A split transaction is claimed part by part, so the
work half of a shop can be claimed on its own.

**Rules can claim for you:** add the action **Claim as tax deduction** to a rule (for example, payee
contains "Officeworks") and matching payments are claimed as they import. A rule never replaces a
claim you made yourself.

## Tax time

**Tax time** shows one financial year (1 July to 30 June) at a time: the total claimed, the total
by label, and every claim with its cost, work-use share and receipts. It points out what a tax
agent would ask about:

- **No receipt:** once your work-related expenses pass $300 for the year, the ATO expects written
  evidence for each of them.
- **Over $300:** an item costing more than $300 is usually claimed as decline in value over several
  years rather than all at once.

**Deductions (CSV)** downloads the list; **CSV and receipts (ZIP)** adds every receipt file, named
by date, label and payee. Keep receipts for five years from when you lodge your return.
